How it works

A spin-off is a company split out of a bigger parent and handed to the parent's shareholders. Many get sold off by index funds and big holders in the first weeks, and the backtest found buying after that and holding for a year beat the market on average. Results vary a lot from one spin-off to the next.

The rules

  1. A company files a Form 10-12B with the SEC and starts trading on a US exchange (NYSE, Nasdaq, NYSE American or Cboe) within a year. Its first trading day is day 0.
  2. Buy at the open on US trading day 21, if over the previous 20 trading days it closed at $1 or more and traded at least $100,000 a day on average. You have 5 trading days to buy. After that it counts as missed.
  3. Each buy gets 1/8 of your whole account (your risk level in Settings). Pay for it by selling the index fund. If the fund doesn't cover it, sell all of the fund and trim your other spin-offs in proportion to their value.
  4. Sell at the open 252 US trading days (about 12 months) after the day you bought. No other exits.
  5. Everything else sits in the index fund, including your monthly deposits and sale proceeds.

What the page does

A scheduled check scans SEC filings and prices every weekday morning and writes the results here. This page turns them into today's actions and compares your account with putting the same deposits into an S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 fund. It counts every pound that isn't in a spin-off as held in your index fund. If you choose EQQQ, reinvest its dividends so the numbers match.

Times are UK time. The US market opens at 14:30 UK time for most of the year and 13:30 for a few weeks in March and late October to early November, when the clocks change on different dates. Trading 212 charges 0.15% to convert pounds to dollars.
